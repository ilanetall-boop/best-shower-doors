Things to Know Before You Buy

  • Sliding doors need no swing clearance. All seven doors here run on a track rather than a hinge, which is why sliding styles show up so often on any list of shower doors for small spaces where floor space in front of the stall is limited.
  • Measure the rough opening, not the old door. Most of these doors list an adjustable width range, such as 55 to 60 inches or 56 to 60 inches, so measure your actual opening at a few heights before ordering.
  • The base or pan is rarely included. Every door on this list ships as glass and hardware only, so budget separately for the shower pan or curb underneath it.
  • Frameless glass reads larger in a tight stall. A door without a thick metal frame lets more light through the enclosure, which helps a small bathroom feel less boxed in even at the same width.
  • Finish affects price more than size does. Matte black and stainless steel hardware both add cost over a plain chrome finish, part of why prices here range from $249.30 to $449.96 across similar widths.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best shower door for a small space?

Among the seven doors we compared, the Aurisal frameless stainless steel sliding door is our pick for most small bathrooms. It fits a 60 inch by 72 and a quarter inch opening, and its sliding track needs no floor clearance the way a swinging door does.

Do sliding shower doors save space in a small bathroom?

Yes. A sliding or bypass door runs along a fixed track instead of swinging open, so it does not need the clear floor space a pivot or hinged door requires, which is why sliding styles dominate lists of shower doors for small spaces.

How do I measure my bathroom for a small shower door?

Measure the rough wall to wall opening at a few different heights, since older stalls are rarely perfectly square, then compare that number against each door's adjustable width range, such as 55 to 60 inches or 56 to 60 inches, rather than a single listed size.

Why do two Aurisal doors on this list cost different prices for the same size?

The two Aurisal doors we compared, priced at $399.99 and $389.99, both cover the same 60 inch by 72 and a quarter inch opening with no stated difference in glass or hardware. That kind of gap usually comes down to seller, warehouse or promotion rather than a real product difference, so it is worth checking both listings before you order.

Are frameless shower doors better for small bathrooms than framed ones?

A frameless design tends to suit a small bathroom well because it removes the thick metal border that a framed unit uses to support the glass, which opens up sightlines in a stall that already feels tight. Frameless doors generally use thicker glass to compensate for the missing frame, which is part of why our frameless picks here cost more than a basic framed slider would.
