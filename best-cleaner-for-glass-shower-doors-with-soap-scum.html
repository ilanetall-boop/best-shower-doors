Soap scum builds up faster than most people expect. Bar soap and body wash both contain fatty acids that react with calcium and magnesium in tap water, and that reaction leaves a hazy film that bonds tighter to glass the longer it sits. A fresh layer wipes away easily, but scum that has cured for weeks resists plain water and needs a cleaner formulated to break down that bond instead of scrubbing at the surface forever.

These seven products span $9.97 to $29.95, and six of them remove existing scum while one, the Rain-X 630542 Shower Door Water, is meant to run after your glass is already clean to keep new residue from forming. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best cleaner for glass shower doors with soap scum?
For most bathrooms, the Rain-X 630544 X-Treme Clean Shower is the best cleaner for glass shower doors with soap scum, because it breaks down soap film and body-wash residue without a scrub brush, and a two-bottle pack costs $16.00. If scum has built into a thicker film, the GlasWeld ProClean or UNGER Commercial RubOut give you a stronger formula to work with.
Why does soap scum stick to shower glass in the first place?
Soap scum forms when the fatty acids in bar soap and body wash react with minerals in your water and bond to the glass. The longer it sits, the harder that film gets, which is why a cleaner built for soap scum works faster than wiping with plain water or a general bathroom spray.
Do I need a different cleaner for soap scum versus hard water stains?
Soap scum and hard water stains often show up together, but they come from different sources, soap residue in one case and mineral deposits in the other. Several products in this comparison, including Uncle Todd's and Brite & Clean, market themselves for both soap scum and hard water buildup, so you don't necessarily need two separate bottles.
