Things to Know Before You Buy

  • Measure your rough opening at the top, middle and bottom before you start a shower door installation. The doors in this guide cover widths from 24 inches to 60 inches, and using anything but your smallest measurement can leave a gap or force a return.
  • Sliding doors need wall space on either side of the track, while pivot doors swing open and need floor clearance in front of the stall instead.
  • Every door here uses tempered safety glass, but frame material and hardware finish vary, and the least expensive picks tend to use lighter-gauge aluminum.
  • A corner enclosure fits stalls with two adjoining walls, so measure both walls and the depth of your stall, not just the front opening width.
  • Budget doors under $250 rarely include soft-close hinges or a lifetime warranty, so check the return window before you drill into tile or cut a threshold.

Shower door installation only goes smoothly when the door you order actually matches your opening, your wall type and the space you have to swing or slide it open. Get the width wrong by even half an inch and you are looking at a return, a gap that leaks, or a track that never sits flush against the wall.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I measure my opening before a shower door installation?
Measure the width of your rough opening at the top, middle and bottom, then use the smallest number when comparing listed size ranges. Do the same for height if your stall has a lower ceiling or a tub deck. The doors in this guide cover widths from 24 to 60 inches, and ordering against your largest measurement instead of your smallest is one of the most common causes of a return.
Can I handle a shower door installation myself, or should I hire a professional?
Many of the sliding and pivot doors in this guide are designed for DIY installation with basic tools, and verified owner reviews often describe a straightforward setup when the opening is measured correctly. A frameless corner enclosure like the ComfyStyle pick involves more precise leveling across two walls, so if your walls are not close to plumb, hiring a professional can save you from a door that never sits flush.
What is the difference between sliding, pivot and corner shower doors?
Sliding doors glide along a track and work best for wider openings since the panels need wall space on either side to overlap. Pivot doors swing open like a regular door and need less side clearance, which suits narrower stalls, as long as there is floor space in front for the door to swing. Corner enclosures are built for stalls with two adjoining walls rather than a single straight opening, and they are not interchangeable with either style.
