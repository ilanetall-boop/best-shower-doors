Things to Know Before You Buy

  • Measure your opening width and height before buying. Doors in this roundup range from a 24 inch pivot model to a 60 inch sliding bathtub door, and the wrong size means gaps or a return.
  • Pivot doors suit smaller bathrooms since they swing open rather than slide, while sliding doors save floor space but need a wider rough opening on either side of the track.
  • Every door here uses tempered glass, but thickness and hardware finish vary, and the least expensive models tend to use lighter-gauge aluminum framing.
  • Corner (neo-angle) doors fit stalls with two adjoining walls, while bathtub sliding doors are built with a shorter frame height for a tub deck instead of a full-height stall.
  • Budget doors under $300 rarely include soft-close hinges or lifetime warranties, so check the return policy before you drill into tile.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size shower door do I need?
Measure the width of your rough opening at the top, middle and bottom, then use the smallest number. Most sliding and pivot doors in this price range cover an adjustable range, commonly 44 to 48 inches for standard alcove showers, though pivot and corner doors can run as narrow as 24 inches or as wide as 60 inches for bathtub conversions. Check a listing's stated width range against your smallest measurement before ordering.
Are budget shower doors as durable as premium ones?
Budget doors under $300 use the same tempered safety glass required by code, but they typically skip extras like soft-close hinges, hard-water-resistant coatings and lifetime warranties that premium doors include. Verified owner reviews on the doors in this roundup show most hold up well for daily use, though a few models show a pattern of hardware needing occasional re-adjustment after the first several months.
Should I choose a sliding or pivot shower door?
Sliding doors work best for wider openings, roughly 44 inches and up, since the panels need wall space on either side to overlap. Pivot doors swing open like a regular door and need less side clearance, which makes them a better fit for narrower openings around 24 to 30 inches, as long as your bathroom has floor space in front of the stall for the door to swing.
